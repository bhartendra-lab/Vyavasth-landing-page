Canva doesn't have 100 sections. It has six, and every one of them is loud.

A teardown of canva.com (desktop 1440px and mobile 375px), measured from the live page, and what it means for a 6-section Vyavasth home page in terracotta, brown and peach.

~7,560px tall at desktop5 content blocks + CTA band10 floating hero objects24px card radius5 muted looping videos
The energy on canva.com does not come from volume. The logged-out home page is only hero, tools carousel, two-up bento, template wall, ecosystem bento, CTA band. It comes from six devices used every time: full-bleed colour, a different saturated field per card, real product pieces floating and breaking out of their frames, smiling human faces, named cursors that make it feel inhabited, and motion tied to your scroll. All six carry over to Vyavasth. The rainbow does not.

Section by section

What each block is for, and what's mechanically making it feel alive. Specs were read from the live page's computed styles, not guessed.

01 · Hero

"What will you design today?"

  • Full-bleed vertical gradient that dissolves into the white page: purple #992BFF → violet → teal #13A3B5 → ice → white. No hard section edge.
  • Headline is a question about the visitor's job, not a description of Canva. 80px, regular weight (400), -0.8px tracking. It's loud from size and colour, not boldness.
  • One CTA. White pill, 56px tall, 20px radius. No second button competing.
  • 10 floating objects, and each is a product noun: a text layer, an AI portrait, a heart (social), a voice wave, a colour wheel, a sticky note, the logo, a background-removed ball, a code tag.
  • Scroll-linked parallax: objects drift up 50 to 70px and tilt ±2° over the first 900px of scroll, done with CSS animation-timeline: scroll(root). No JS, cheap on phones.
02 · Tools carousel

"Tools to power your best work"

  • Sticky pill tab bar (AI · Presentations · Social · Video · Print Shop) that stays pinned 16px from the top while you're in the section. Each tab has its own coloured icon.
  • Horizontal carousel of cards in a wide/narrow rhythm: 851px and 413px wide, both 520px tall, 24px radius.
  • Every card is its own saturated field: magenta, coral, pink, sky blue. Neighbouring cards never share a colour.
  • Card recipe: one 32px line + one small white "Explore X" pill, then product UI collaged in the lower half, bleeding off the card edge.
  • Named cursors ("Felicia", "Léa") pointing at things. It reads as people working in there right now.
02 · cont.

The UI pops out of the card

  • The Magic Write menu floats above a blurred paragraph. The UI is bigger than life, cropped tight, not a full-screen screenshot shrunk down.
  • Photos are people laughing, shot bright, with high saturation.
  • Hover: media inside the card scales to 1.09; buttons scale 1.06 and lift 3px. Hover effects only switch on for mouse users (hover: hover).
03 · Two-up bento

"All the tools. All in one place."

  • Two equal giant cards. One emblem (the Canva logo with orbiting app icons) and one real-use collage (a pitch deck with a real person).
  • Short rhythm break after the busy carousel. Same card recipe, so the page feels like one system.
04 · Template wall

"Templates for absolutely anything"

  • The output is the hero. A two-row masonry of finished designs in mixed aspect ratios, horizontally scrollable, some with a play badge.
  • Here the colour comes from the customers' work, not the UI chrome.
  • Two CTAs here, primary purple plus a ghost "Browse all".
05 · Ecosystem bento

"Unlock Canva's creative ecosystem"

  • 2×2 bento in the same 2:1 wide/narrow rhythm, alternating sides per row (wide-left, then wide-right).
  • One audience per card: learners, fans (licensed IP), enterprise, small business. The segmentation is done in cards, not separate pages.
06 · CTA band

"Start designing with Canva"

  • Full-bleed horizontal gradient (teal → violet → purple), 64px headline, the logo wordmark used as the last word of the sentence.
  • One button. Then the footer.
Mobile

Objects go to the edges, not away

  • On a 375px screen the headline goes to 3 lines and the objects scatter around it, cropped off the screen edges. On desktop they sat in rows below it.
  • The headline also gets shorter subcopy: "Make socials, videos, slides, and more".
  • This matters more for you than for Canva, because your cold Instagram traffic lands here on a phone.

The six energy devices, specced

These are the parts to steal. Each one is translated into the Vyavasth palette and product.

1 · Colour fields

Every block is a colour, not a border

Canva never puts a pale card on a pale page. Each card is filled. For Vyavasth: terracotta, deep brown, peach, dark terracotta and the lilac-pink #EECEEC rotate so no two neighbours match.

radius 24px · neighbours never share a fill
2 · Product objects

Floating nouns, not icons

Each floating object is a real piece of the product. For Vyavasth: the selfie ring, "128 photos found", a WhatsApp ping, a 5-star review, a QR, a RAW file chip, the passcode lock.

8 to 10 objects · parallax 50 to 70px · tilt ±2°
3 · Breakout UI

UI bigger than life, cropped

Show one moment of the UI, oversized, spilling off the card edge, instead of a full dashboard screenshot shrunk to a thumbnail.

UI sits in the lower 55% of the card, bleeds off 1 to 2 edges
4 · Joy

Faces, laughing, bright

Canva's photos are people mid-laugh in daylight. The current Vyavasth hero uses dark, moody stock event shots. Indian weddings are the most colourful content there is: haldi yellow, mehendi green, sangeet lights.

real studio photos · bright · faces in frame
5 · Presence

Named cursors → named faces

Canva's "Felicia" and "Matt" cursors show people working inside the product. Your version comes from the product itself: face-tag chips on photos ("Dadi", "Rohan", "Bua ji") show face search and the family at the same time.

pill chip + pointer, brand colour, 1 to 2 per card
6 · Motion

Motion follows the scroll

Scroll-linked parallax in the hero, muted looping videos that play only when on screen, a carousel, and small hover lifts. All of it is CSS where possible. Browsers that don't support it just show things standing still.

animation-timeline: scroll() · video muted loop, play in view

Where copying Canva would hurt

Friction, as asked for. These are the cracks to design around.

Canva sells a habit. You sell a switch.

Canva's visitor already knows Canva and has nothing to lose by clicking. A studio owner comparing you against Kwikpic or FotoOwl needs proof, a price and the switching story. A pure vibes page will look great and convert worse. Energy and proof have to share the page: that's why section 5 below exists.

One hue family can turn muddy

Canva's energy leans on purple and teal, which sit on opposite sides of the colour wheel. Brown → terracotta → peach all sit in one family. Run as a gradient, it can read as rust or a 2016 Instagram filter. The fix is hard contrast (deep brown next to peach), the lilac #EECEEC as the one cool note, and letting the wedding photos carry the extra colour. Needs a visual test before committing.

No colourful photos, no concept

Canva's page gets its colour from its template wall. Yours has to come from real wedding galleries. If you can't get 30 to 40 bright, permissioned photos from client studios, the whole direction falls back to beige. This is the biggest asset dependency.

Tier-2 Android on 4G

Cold Instagram traffic means mid-range phones. Canva gets away with heavy media because it lazy-loads and uses CSS-only motion. Budget: hero under ~250KB, objects as SVG or WebP, videos only start when on screen, and nothing in the hero waits for JS.

Floating objects can look like clip-art

Generic 3D icons (a camera, a cloud, a sparkle) will look like every AI SaaS from 2024. They only work if each one is a recognisable piece of your UI.

Studio marquee

The marquee lists "Golden Hour Studio", "Pixel Bloom", "Studio Kohinoor"… If these aren't real customers, they're a credibility risk on a page meant to build trust. Real names only, or drop it.

Palette, translated

Same theme colours, used as fills instead of accents. The hero gradient follows Canva's dark-to-page-colour dissolve.
