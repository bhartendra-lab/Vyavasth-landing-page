Most wedding studios in India still work on a WhatsApp message and a handshake. It works until it does not: a family disputes the balance, a couple demands RAW files, or a date shifts after the advance is spent. A one-page written agreement, signed or accepted on WhatsApp before the advance, prevents most of these fights.

This is a practical checklist, not legal advice. For anything high-value or unusual, have a lawyer review your template once.

## The short answer

Put eight things in writing before you take the advance: who the client is, the dates and functions covered, the team, the deliverables with dates, the payment schedule, what happens if the wedding is postponed or cancelled, the RAW file policy, and who owns the photos and how each side may use them.

## 1. Client and event details

- Names and phone numbers of the person paying and the couple.
- Every function covered, with date, venue and hours. "Wedding coverage" is not enough when there are five functions over three days.
- What happens if a function runs over time: an hourly rate, or included up to a limit.

## 2. Team and equipment

- How many photographers and videographers, and who leads.
- Drone, candid, traditional, cinematic: list what is included, not just the package name.

## 3. Deliverables and dates

This is where most disputes start. Be specific:

| Deliverable | Write down |
|---|---|
| Preview photos | How many, and by when |
| Full edited set | Approximate number, format, and delivery date |
| Album | Sheets, size, type, and the selection deadline for the couple |
| Videos | Teaser, highlight film and full film, with lengths and dates |
| Online gallery | How long it stays live |

Tie the album date to the couple's selection date: "Album delivered 30 days after selection is final." Then a slow selection does not count against you. More on that in [why album selection takes months](/blog/why-album-selection-takes-months).

## 4. Payment schedule

- Advance amount, and that it confirms the booking.
- Second instalment, usually on the wedding day or before the first function.
- Balance, and whether the full set or album is released only after it is paid.
- Whether GST is included or extra, and that you will issue an invoice.
- Accepted modes: UPI, bank transfer, card.

## 5. Postponement and cancellation

- **Postponement:** whether the advance moves to a new date, and within how long.
- **Cancellation by the client:** how much of the advance is refundable, if any, and by when.
- **Cancellation by the studio:** what you refund, and whether you arrange a replacement team.
- **Events outside anyone's control:** what happens if the event cannot take place at all.

## 6. RAW files

Couples increasingly ask for RAW files. Decide your policy and write it down: not included, included, or available for an extra fee. If you do not say anything, you will be asked at the worst possible moment.

## 7. Who owns the photos, and how they can be used

This surprises many studios. Under India's Copyright Act, when a photograph is taken for payment at the request of a client, the client is the first owner of the copyright unless there is an agreement to the contrary (Section 17(b)). In other words, without a written clause, the couple may own the photos you took.

If you want to keep the copyright and give the couple a licence for personal use, or simply keep the right to use selected photos on your Instagram and website, say so in the contract. Many studios also add that they will not use photos the couple asks them to keep private.

## 8. Delivery and storage

- How photos will be delivered: online gallery, pen drive, or both.
- How long you keep a backup after delivery, so families know when to download their copies.

## How to get it signed without making it awkward

- Keep it to one or two pages in plain language. Families sign what they can read.
- Send it with the booking confirmation and ask for a "Yes, agreed" reply on WhatsApp if a signature is not practical.
- Use the same template for every wedding, so nothing depends on memory.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a wedding photographer need a written contract in India?

It is not mandatory, but a short written agreement prevents most disputes over payments, deliverables, delivery dates and RAW files. Agreement on WhatsApp is better than nothing.

### Who owns the copyright of wedding photos in India?

Under Section 17(b) of the Copyright Act, when photos are taken for payment at the client's request, the client is the first owner unless the agreement says otherwise. Studios that want to keep the copyright or use photos in their portfolio should state it in the contract.

### Should I give RAW files to the client?

That is your choice, but write it down: not included, included, or available for an extra fee. Deciding upfront avoids disputes later.

### What should happen if the wedding is postponed?

Write your policy in advance: whether the advance moves to the new date, within what period, and what happens if the new date clashes with another booking.
