Most Indian studios still deliver wedding photos on a pen drive, share a preview on Google Drive, and handle selection over WhatsApp. That works until a family of 400 wants their photos on their phones the same week, and the couple takes three months to choose album photos from a folder of 6,000 files.

There are three real options in 2026, and most studios end up using two of them together. Here is what each one is good at and where it breaks.

## The short answer

- **Pen drive or hard drive:** best as the final archive copy the couple keeps. Worst for sharing, selection and getting the photos to relatives.
- **Google Drive:** free to start and everyone knows it. Breaks on storage, on large downloads, and gives your studio no credit for the work.
- **Online gallery built for photographers:** best for sharing with the whole family, album selection and getting reviews. Costs a subscription or a per-event fee.

The setup that works for most studios: deliver through an online gallery during and after the wedding, run album selection inside it, and hand over a pen drive with the final edited photos at the end.

## Pen drive: still the final copy, not the delivery

Families in Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh still expect a pen drive, and there is nothing wrong with that. It is a physical thing they can keep in the album box. The problems start when the pen drive is the only way photos reach anyone.

- **One copy, one house.** The pen drive goes to the couple. The bride's mama, the groom's college friends and the 300 other guests get photos only if someone forwards them on WhatsApp, which compresses them.
- **Selection becomes a file-number list.** The couple scrolls through folders on a laptop and sends back "DSC_4512, DSC_4518..." as a WhatsApp message or an Excel sheet. Someone at the studio then searches for each file by hand.
- **No credit for the studio.** Photos that travel on WhatsApp carry no studio name. The guest who loved the photos does not know who took them.

Use the pen drive for what it is good at: the final edited set, handed over with the album.

## Google Drive: free to start, expensive in time

Google Drive is where most studios move after the pen drive. It is familiar and a link is easy to share. It also creates new problems once you deliver more than a few weddings a season.

- **Storage runs out fast.** The free 15 GB fills up with a single wedding. After that you pay for Google storage every month and keep deleting old weddings to make room.
- **Downloads are painful on phones.** A large folder downloads as several zip files. Most relatives open the link on a phone, see thousands of thumbnails, and give up.
- **Nobody finds their own photos.** A guest who wants the photos they are in has to scroll the entire wedding.
- **Selection is still manual.** Drive has no way for the couple to mark album photos that the studio can then filter. You are back to file-number lists.
- **Your name is nowhere.** The link shows a Google folder, not your studio.

## Online gallery: built for how Indian weddings actually work

An online gallery made for photographers fixes the problems above because it was designed for them. The details differ between tools, but the useful ones do four things:

1. **One link or QR for the whole wedding.** Put it on a standee at the venue or send it to the family WhatsApp group.
2. **Face search.** Each guest takes a selfie and sees only the photos they are in. Nobody scrolls 6,000 photos.
3. **Album selection by likes.** The couple and family like photos on their phones. The studio filters those likes and gets a clean shortlist instead of a list of file numbers.
4. **Your studio's name on every screen.** Logo, watermark, Instagram link and a request for a Google review, shown to every relative who opens the gallery.

The cost is a yearly plan or a per-event fee. For a studio doing even 10 weddings a season, the time saved on selection alone usually pays for it.

## A setup that works for most studios

1. **Before the wedding:** create the event in your gallery tool and share the link or QR with the family.
2. **During the wedding:** upload batches after each function, so relatives see Haldi photos before the Sangeet starts.
3. **After the wedding:** the couple and family like photos for the album. You shortlist from their likes, then [pull the matching originals](/blog/find-raw-files-from-client-selection) for your editor.
4. **At handover:** give the album and a pen drive with the final edited photos.

This keeps what families like about the pen drive, and removes the months of back-and-forth around it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it still okay to deliver wedding photos on a pen drive?

Yes, as the final copy the couple keeps. It is not a good way to share photos with relatives or to run album selection, because every photo has to be forwarded by hand and selection turns into lists of file numbers.

### Why do my Google Drive links not work well for clients?

Large wedding folders download as several zip files, phones struggle with thousands of thumbnails, and guests cannot find the photos they are in. Drive also has no way to mark album selections that you can filter later.

### How do guests find their own photos in an online gallery?

Galleries with face search ask each guest for one selfie and then show only the photos that person appears in. Guests do not need to scroll the whole wedding.

### What should I give the couple at the end?

The printed album and a pen drive or hard drive with the final edited photos. Keep the online gallery live for a few months so relatives can still find and download their photos.
