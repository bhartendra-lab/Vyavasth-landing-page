Same-day delivery means guests see their photos during the wedding, not weeks later. For Indian weddings with several functions over two or three days, it has become one of the easiest ways for a studio to stand out. It does not need a bigger team. It needs a clear setup.

## The short answer

One person handles uploads while the others shoot. Photos go up in batches after each function, from the camera, a laptop or a phone. A gallery with face search lets every guest find their own photos as each batch lands.

## Who does what

| Role | Job during the event |
|---|---|
| Lead photographer | Shoots the key moments, as usual |
| Second shooter | Covers guests, candids and the other side of the stage |
| Upload person | Collects cards, uploads batches, checks the gallery, answers family questions |

In a small studio the upload person can be an assistant or a junior. Their only job is to make sure nothing waits on a card.

## Three ways to get photos up

1. **From the camera over FTP.** Many professional cameras can send photos to a server over Wi-Fi or a phone hotspot as they are taken. Fastest, but it needs setup and testing before the wedding day.
2. **From a laptop.** Swap cards, copy to the laptop, and upload through the gallery's web uploader. Works with any camera.
3. **From a phone over USB.** Some tools have an Android app that takes photos from the camera by cable and uploads them on mobile data.

## Venue internet: plan for the worst

- Carry your own hotspot with a good data plan; venue Wi-Fi is often shared with hundreds of guests.
- Upload compressed copies for viewing, and keep originals on your cards and drives.
- Use an uploader that resumes on its own when the connection drops, so a weak signal slows you down instead of losing photos.
- Upload between functions, when the network is quieter.

## A timeline for a three-function wedding

| When | Upload |
|---|---|
| Haldi ends | First batch: 100 to 200 photos, the best of the function |
| Before Sangeet | Rest of Haldi |
| Sangeet break | First Sangeet batch |
| Late night | Rest of Sangeet |
| Wedding day, after varmala | Varmala and stage photos |
| After pheras | Ceremony highlights |
| Next morning | Everything else |

You do not need to upload everything live. A strong first batch after each function is what guests remember.

## What changes for the studio

- Guests find their photos the same day, with your name on every screen.
- The couple can start liking photos for the album the same week. See [why album selection takes months](/blog/why-album-selection-takes-months).
- Relatives at the wedding become your next enquiries.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is same-day delivery possible without camera Wi-Fi?

Yes. Copying cards to a laptop and uploading through a web uploader works with any camera. Camera FTP is faster but optional.

### How many photos should be uploaded during the event?

A selected first batch after each function is enough. Upload the rest later the same night or the next day.

### What if the venue internet is slow?

Use your own hotspot, upload compressed copies for viewing, and use an uploader that resumes on its own after a dropped connection.

### Do I need an extra person for same-day delivery?

It helps to have one person responsible for uploads. In a small team it can be an assistant, so the photographers keep shooting.
