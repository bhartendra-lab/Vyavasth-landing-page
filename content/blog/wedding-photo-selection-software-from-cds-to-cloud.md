Every wedding album in India has the same hidden step between the wedding and the printing press: someone has to choose which 300 of 8,000 photos go in. How studios do that has changed more in fifteen years than the cameras have. This is the short history, with a few tools you may still find in a studio cupboard.

## Era 1: the studio visit

The original method needed no software at all. The family came to the studio, sat in front of the computer, and the studio's selection person opened photos one by one.

We saw this still happening recently. Two women from the bride's family came to a studio, late and after several reminders. A staff member opened each photo in the image viewer, remembered the file name of every one they liked, then searched the folder and copied each original by hand.

It worked, slowly. It needed the family to travel, a staff member for a full afternoon, and someone with a very good memory for file names.

## Era 2: selection software on a disc

Then came Windows software that let families choose at home. A good example is **AJANTA ROYAL Select and Sort**, made by Ajanta Photo and Technologies in Nagpur and still listed on Amazon India today at ₹999, first available in September 2019. Its listing describes the workflow of the era:

- The studio exports a **"protected package file"** of the wedding photos that the family "cannot copy or print".
- The family opens it in a special photo viewer, selects photos and adds comments.
- The studio can **lock the package** after a number of days or views, so "party is unable to see their photos after time limit".
- When the selected file comes back, the software **"magically sorts"** the selected originals for the studio in minutes.

It runs on Windows 7, 8 and 10 (64-bit) with 2 GB of RAM, for one user.

Credit where it is due: the problems it solved are the same ones studios have today. Families should be able to choose at home. Studios should not lose control of their photos. And nobody should match file names by hand. The tool even sorted originals automatically, years before cloud galleries did.

The limits were the limits of the era. The package travelled on a pen drive. It needed a Windows computer at the family's home. And one family member chose for everyone, because the file sat on one laptop.

Album design software from the same era came the same way. DgFlick Album Xpress was sold on CD/DVD with "a lifetime of free upgrades", and Karizma albums still ships its software on a USB dongle. You paid once, installed it on the studio PC, and it was yours.

## Era 3: WhatsApp screenshots and Google Drive

Smartphones and cheap data broke the pen drive habit. Studios started uploading weddings to Google Drive and sharing the link in the family WhatsApp group. Families chose by sending screenshots, or file numbers typed into a message.

It was free and everyone already knew how to use it. It also created a new job: someone in the studio decoding forty screenshots from six relatives, half of them of the same photo, then finding each file on the drive. WhatsApp compressed every photo along the way, so families were choosing from blurry copies. And a popular Drive link can hit Google's download limits right when the whole family opens it. ## Era 4: cloud galleries with likes

Online client galleries moved selection into the browser. International tools like Pixieset let clients mark favourites and leave comments, and Indian tools added face search so each guest could find their own photos with a selfie.

Likes fixed the screenshot problem: the studio could see exactly which photo was chosen. But likes have no destination. Liking 500 photos still leaves someone to decide which 300 go in the bride's album, which in the groom's, and which on the calendar. So the back and forth moved from WhatsApp into the gallery.

## Era 5: collections, filters and one-click originals

The newest generation of tools treats album selection as a job of its own:

- **Collections** for each album, calendar or table book, so a photo has a destination, and one photo can sit in several.
- **A small selection team** chosen by the couple, while everyone else can still like photos.
- **Filters** by the bride's side, the groom's side, by person and by total likes.
- **One click to the originals**: the studio's laptop finds the original and RAW files of the final selection and copies them into one folder for the editor.

That last one is the descendant of Select and Sort's "magic sort", now running from a cloud gallery instead of a package on a pen drive. This is how Vyavasth works, and you can see it on the [album selection feature page](/features/album-selection).

## What changed, in one table

| Era | How families chose | What the studio did | Main pain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio visit | Sat with staff at the studio | Remembered file names, copied by hand | Travel, staff time |
| Software on a disc | Protected file on a Windows PC at home | Sorted originals with the software | One laptop, one chooser, pen drives |
| WhatsApp and Drive | Screenshots and file numbers | Decoded screenshots, searched folders | Compression, confusion |
| Cloud galleries with likes | Liked photos on the phone | Guessed the final cut from the likes | Likes have no destination |
| Collections and one-click originals | Built a collection per album | Shortlisted and pulled originals in one click | Getting families to start |

## What has not changed

Three things are the same in every era: the family wants a say, the studio wants control of its photos, and the album waits until the selection is done. The tools only change how long that wait is. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is AJANTA ROYAL Select and Sort?

A Windows photo selection program for wedding and event photographers, made by Ajanta Photo and Technologies in Nagpur and sold on Amazon India for ₹999. The studio exports a protected package that the family opens, selects from and sends back, and the software sorts the selected originals.

### Is offline photo selection software still useful?

For studios whose clients have little internet access, yes. For most families today, a phone gallery link is easier than installing software on a Windows computer, and it lets several relatives choose at once.

### What is the best way for couples to select album photos today?

Create one collection per album or product, let a small selection team fill them function by function, and share them with the studio as you go. The studio then pulls the originals of the final selection in one click.
