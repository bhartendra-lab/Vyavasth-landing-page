Google Drive is how most Indian studios started delivering online, and for good reason. Everyone already has a Google account, it costs nothing to begin, and the link fits in a WhatsApp message.

It is also a file store, not a wedding gallery. That difference stays invisible until the night 300 relatives open the same link. This post lists the six limits studios hit most, and the cases where Drive is still the right tool.

**Disclosure:** we make Vyavasth, an online gallery for wedding studios. We use Google Drive ourselves, for files.

## 1. Popular files get locked for a while

When many people view or download the same shared file in a short time, Drive can block it and show: "Sorry, you can't view or download this file at this time. Too many users have viewed or downloaded this file recently."

Google does not publish the exact limit. Users and IT forums commonly report that the block lifts after about 24 hours. For a wedding, that is the worst possible timing: the link is popular exactly when the family is sharing it in every group.

## 2. Free storage runs out after a few weddings

A free Google account has 15 GB, shared between Gmail, Drive and Google Photos. A single wedding of 4,000 edited JPEGs at 5 to 8 MB each is 20 to 30 GB. So a studio delivering on Drive pays for Google One within the first wedding, and for 2 TB within the first season.

That is fine as a cost. The catch is that the storage belongs to one Google account, often the owner's personal one, and every wedding the studio ever delivered lives inside it.

## 3. On a phone, Drive feels like a file manager

Most guests open the link on a phone, from WhatsApp. They see a grid of file names like DSC_4471.JPG, folders inside folders, and a prompt to open the Drive app. There is no cover photo, no studio branding and no order by function unless the studio built folders carefully.

Guests who cannot find themselves in 4,000 photos in the first minute usually stop looking.

## 4. Downloading a whole folder gives you several zip files

When someone downloads a large folder, Drive packs it into zip files and splits big downloads into several parts. On a phone, that means a folder of zips in Downloads that many relatives do not know how to open, and photos that never reach the gallery app.

## 5. No way for guests to find their own photos

Drive has no face search. Each guest scrolls every folder looking for themselves. At a 400-guest wedding, that is the main thing guests want, and the one thing Drive cannot do. For how selfie search works and what to check, see [AI face search for wedding photos: 8 things to check](/blog/ai-face-search-photo-sharing-what-to-check).

## 6. Album selection turns into screenshots

Drive has no likes and no shortlist. Comments need a Google account and are hard to follow across 4,000 files. So families choose album photos by sending screenshots or file numbers on WhatsApp, and someone in the studio decodes them. More on this in [album selection on Excel, WhatsApp or an app](/blog/album-selection-excel-whatsapp-or-app).

## When Google Drive is still the right tool

Drive is good at what it was built for:

- **Sending RAW files or full-resolution originals** to one person, like an editor or an album designer.
- **Backups** of the studio's own work, alongside a second copy on a hard disk.
- **Small shoots** like a portrait session or a product shoot for one client, where only one person opens the link.
- **Video files** to a single client, where a gallery adds nothing.

The rule of thumb: Drive works when one person opens the link. It struggles when the whole wedding does.

## Moving from Drive to a gallery without changing everything

You do not have to drop Drive. Most studios that switch keep Drive for originals and backups, and use a gallery only for what guests see:

1. Keep exporting edited JPEGs as you do today.
2. Upload them to the gallery by function (haldi, mehendi, wedding, reception).
3. Share one gallery link or a QR code at the venue. See [QR code for wedding photos](/blog/qr-code-for-wedding-photos).
4. Keep RAW files and originals on Drive or a hard disk for the album designer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does Google Drive say "too many users have viewed or downloaded this file"?

Drive limits how often a shared file can be viewed or downloaded in a short time. When a wedding link is shared widely, popular files can hit that limit and stay blocked for a while, often reported as about 24 hours. Google does not publish the exact numbers.

### How much Google Drive storage does a wedding need?

It depends on photo count and file size. 4,000 edited JPEGs at 5 to 8 MB each come to roughly 20 to 30 GB, which is more than the 15 GB free on a Google account.

### Is Google Drive good for sending wedding photos to clients?

For sending files to one person, like the couple or an album designer, yes. For sharing a whole wedding with hundreds of guests on their phones, a gallery built for weddings works better, because it handles many viewers, face search and album selection.

### Can guests find their own photos in Google Drive?

Not directly. Drive has no face search, so guests scroll through every folder. Wedding gallery apps let a guest take a selfie and see every photo they appear in.
