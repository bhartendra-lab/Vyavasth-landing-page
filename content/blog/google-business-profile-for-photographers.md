When a couple in a small city searches "wedding photographer near me", the first thing they see is not a website. It is a map with three studios on it, each with a star rating, photos and a call button. That box comes from Google Business Profile, it is free, and for many Indian studios it brings in more enquiries than Instagram.

This guide covers setting one up properly, the mistakes that get profiles suspended, and how to keep it growing after every wedding you deliver. We last checked Google's own help pages on 18 November 2026; Google changes the steps often, so follow what the screen shows if it differs.

## The short answer

1. Go to business.google.com and add your studio with its real name, no extra keywords.
2. Pick **Wedding photographer** or **Photographer** as your main category.
3. If clients come to your studio, show the address. If you only travel to venues, hide it and add service areas.
4. Verify, usually by recording a short video.
5. Add photos, services and hours, then ask every client for a review when you deliver their photos.

## Step 1: create the profile

1. Open business.google.com and sign in with the Google account you want to own the profile. Use the studio's Google Workspace email if you have one, not a personal Gmail, so the profile stays with the business if staff change.
2. Search for your studio name. If it already exists (Google sometimes creates profiles from Maps data), claim it instead of making a duplicate.
3. Enter the business name **exactly as it appears on your signboard, visiting card and Instagram**.

**Do not add keywords to the name.** "Rahul Studio" is right. "Rahul Studio Best Wedding Photographer Bhopal" breaks Google's guidelines, competitors can report it, and it is a common reason profiles get suspended. Put your keywords in the description and services instead.

## Step 2: choose the right categories

Your main category decides which searches you appear in. For most Indian wedding studios:

| If you mainly... | Main category | Add as extra categories |
|---|---|---|
| Shoot weddings and events | Wedding photographer | Photographer, Videographer |
| Run a walk-in studio (passport photos, portraits) | Photography studio | Photographer, Wedding photographer |
| Shoot many kinds of work | Photographer | Wedding photographer, Photography studio |

Do not add categories just as keywords. Google's guidelines say categories should describe your business, not features of it.

## Step 3: address or service area (and how to hide a home address)

This is where most home-based photographers get stuck.

- **You have a studio clients visit, with a signboard:** show the address. You can also add service areas.
- **You work from home and only travel to venues:** you are a "service-area business". Google's rule is that if you don't serve customers at your address, you remove it from the profile. Google still needs it for verification, but it is not shown publicly.
- **Service areas:** add the cities or districts you cover, by name or PIN code (not a radius). Google allows up to 20 service areas and says the whole area should be within about 2 hours' drive of your base. So a Bhopal studio can list Bhopal, Sehore, Vidisha and Raisen, but not Mumbai because you once shot a wedding there.

## Step 4: verify

Google decides which verification methods you get; you cannot choose. The common ones in India now are:

- **Video recording (most common):** a short, continuous video showing your location signage or street, your equipment or workspace, and proof you manage the business, such as opening your studio's Google account or a business document. Record in one take, in daylight, without editing.
- **Live video call** with a Google support person during business hours.
- **Phone or SMS code**, or **email**, for some businesses.
- **Postcard** with a code, which can take up to 14 days and expires after 30.

Reviews of verification can take up to 5 business days. While a postcard code is on its way, do not change the name, address or category, or the code stops working.

## Step 5: fill everything in

A complete profile ranks better and converts better. Spend one evening on this:

- **Description:** what you shoot, where, and since when, in plain words. This is where "wedding photographer in Bhopal" belongs.
- **Services:** wedding photography, pre-wedding shoots, candid photography, cinematic video, albums, with starting prices if you are comfortable sharing them.
- **Photos:** 20 to 30 of your best, plus your team and studio. Add new photos after each season.
- **Hours:** if clients visit, real hours. If not, the hours you answer calls.
- **WhatsApp and website:** add your website and a WhatsApp chat link so enquiries land where you already reply.

## Step 6: reviews, the part that actually moves you up

Google says local results are ranked on relevance, distance and prominence, and reviews (how many, how good, how recent) are a big part of prominence. Most studios get very few because they ask once, by message, weeks after the wedding, when the client has moved on.

The best moment to ask is when the client is happiest: when their photos arrive. That is why Vyavasth shows a Google review prompt inside every gallery, so guests and the family see it while they are looking at their photos. More on this in [how to get more Google reviews for your photography studio](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-photography-studio) and on the [Google reviews feature page](/features/google-reviews-for-photographers).

Reply to every review, good or bad, within a few days. A calm, specific reply to a bad review reassures the next couple more than ten five-star reviews do.

## Changing your name or address later

- **Name change:** edit it from the profile, and only to match a real change in your branding. Google may ask you to verify again.
- **Moving the studio:** update the address. Google says a business that moves after verification must verify again.
- **Going from studio to home-based:** remove the address and add service areas instead.

Make one change at a time and wait for it to be approved before the next. Several changes at once are more likely to trigger a review.

## Mistakes that get profiles suspended

- Keywords in the business name.
- A home address shown publicly when clients never visit it.
- Two profiles for the same studio.
- A virtual office or a friend's shop as your address.
- Buying reviews, or offering discounts for five stars. Google removes them and can restrict the profile.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Google Business Profile free for photographers?

Yes. Creating and running a Google Business Profile is free. Google Ads are separate and paid.

### Can I create a Google Business Profile without a shop?

Yes. If you work from home and travel to venues, set it up as a service-area business, hide your address and list the cities you cover.

### Which category should a wedding photographer choose?

Wedding photographer as the main category, with Photographer and Videographer as extra categories if you also do those.

### How do I get my studio to show up on Google Maps?

Create and verify a Google Business Profile with an accurate address or service area. Once verified, it can appear on Google Maps and in local search results.

### How do I get more Google reviews for my studio?

Ask at the moment the client sees their photos, make it one tap, and ask every client, not just the ones you think will be happy. A review prompt inside the photo gallery does this automatically.
